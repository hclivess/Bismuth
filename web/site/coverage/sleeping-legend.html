What is Bismuth(BIS)? What is your Proposal?

Bismuth is a cryptocurrency project focused on innovation and user features, where its goal is to provide a chain, a formidable and futuristic ecosystem, from a new code base. Its philosophy of creation is based on the Principle of the Real World, Storage and Trust, where according to the developers without these three pillars the lack of meaning and adherence will be eminent due to the lack of connection and need. What do you mean? When we think about the real world, we realize that dreams and idealism are connected to different needs, right? So creating something new and without a lack of use is a natural problem that, if not solved, will result in failure. With this in mind, Bismuth provides its platform in Python, a high-level programming language, interpreted script, imperative, object-oriented, functional, dynamically typed and strong, where through its logical lines it provides everything you need to send, receive, hold and other resources found on its platform. Now when we think about storage, the Bismuth project provides its own way of storing, i.e. being unique in several sub-segments, and storage is no different. According to its essence, storage is only about what needs to be stored in the main chain and nothing else. In practice, Bismuth focuses on the necessary data, where this line provides high scalability due to the high need to balance speed, security and storage. The closer these paradigms are to each other, the higher the quality and scalability of the project.

Concluding my reflections on the three pillars, in my opinion, trust is built from its inception to its delivery. So providing transparency, quality and objectivity is the conclusion of true trust. Users of different levels will project their needs onto a project, right? Yes, and for that to happen, boldness, creativity and willingness connect with the practicality of the project. What does this mean in practice? For Bismuth, the abstract connection of daring, creativity and will connect with the practicality of transparency, quality and objectivity, so that the developers’ projections are projected from the sending and receiving of an ordinary user to the creation of protocols for an advanced user. According to Bismuth’s practical interface, we can find a proposal of lightness, value and connectivity. Overall, users of all levels can use and interact with the platform and its ecosystem as a whole. Looking for a node? You’ll find it easy to connect and it doesn’t require great computing power. It can serve as a study tool for developers and beginners where they can experiment and all this is available in multiple layers of access with user APIs from different parts of the world and languages.

The general essence of Bismuth is defined by providing a new blockchain protocol and a platform for real and casual use, where it has an approach that connects innovation and practicality, with a refined database design, introducing its concept of private protocols and contracts, keeping them running and publicly verifiable. At its core, Bismuth is the first blockchain in Python that facilitates the utility of crypto on a large scale.

The Magic of Using Python and SHA224!!!

We know that Python is a programming language widely used in web applications, machine learning(ML), software development and data science. Therefore, this language connected completely with the ideals of the Bismuth developers, where they found the magic needed to design the entire base structure of Bismuth. This is because Python is efficient and easy to use. So when using a database-oriented language and refining it, what would be the best algorithm to connect this ideal? What would? The answer is SHA224, where its technical description can be summed up as a widely used and considered secure algorithm for encryption and data integrity.

As an encryption algorithm, SHA224 converts a data entry into a 224-bit hash and is used to guarantee data integrity and authenticity. In practice, SHA224 generates a unique sequence of characters that cannot be reversed, allowing data to be verified without exposing sensitive information and is used in applications that require data protection and authentication, such as digital signatures, message authentication and secure password storage.
